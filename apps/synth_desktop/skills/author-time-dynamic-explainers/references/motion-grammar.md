# Motion grammar

## Storyboard pattern

Use three to seven beats. A strong default is:

1. Establish the initial object and constraint.
2. Introduce the event, input, or pressure.
3. Perform the mechanism's defining transformation.
4. Reveal the consequence or decision.
5. Settle into the new state and connect it to the next iteration.

Do not force this sequence when the mechanism calls for a comparison, traversal,
or zoom across scales.

## Transition vocabulary

- **Reveal:** introduce a new object only when it becomes relevant.
- **Translate:** move data or control along a causal path.
- **Split:** turn one object into partitions while preserving lineage.
- **Replicate:** duplicate an object and retain a visible origin.
- **Resize:** show quantity or scale through area or length.
- **Reorder:** show sorting, ranking, or priority change through position.
- **Restyle:** mark a state decision after the action, not instead of it.
- **Replace scene:** cut to a new scale or abstraction when continuity would
  create clutter.

## Timing

- Hold the before state long enough to read.
- Perform one main transformation per beat.
- Settle and hold the after state before advancing.
- Use short easing for physical continuity; use step transitions for discrete
  algorithmic decisions.
- Keep the complete explainer bounded. For a compact mechanism, target roughly
  12–30 seconds rather than an endless ambient loop.

## Continuity

- Keep stable objects at stable positions.
- When an object changes identity, show the transition or lineage explicitly.
- Use motion paths that agree with connectors and spatial containment.
- Retain only the context needed to understand the next beat.

## Poster and compact states

- Choose a poster state after the defining transformation, not the busiest frame.
- The poster must communicate the primary claim without playback.
- Treat compact as a distinct composition problem: fewer simultaneous objects,
  shorter labels, focused viewport, or stacked stages. Do not merely scale down
  a wide scene.
